# Meeting: Clarifying Project Goals

## Meeting Details

**Purpose:** Clarify project goals, scope, outcomes, risks, requirements, and timeline for the Sauce & Spoon tabletop menu tablet pilot.

### Attendees

- **Peta** — Project Manager
- **Deanna** — Director of Operations
- **Gilly** — General Manager, North location
- **Alex** — General Manager, Downtown location
- **Seydou** — Restaurant Consultant

---

## Meeting Transcript

**Peta (Project Manager):** Hi, everyone. Thanks for meeting today. I’m so excited to get started and to find out more about what we’re hoping to achieve with this project. I’ve reviewed the existing project documents and I’m in the process of drafting the project charter. Once it’s finalized and approved by all the stakeholders, we can move into the planning stage.

I’d like to take this time to review the information I have so far and make sure we’re off to a successful start.

**Deanna (Director of Operations):** Welcome to the Sauce & Spoon team, Peta! We’re happy to have you. How can we help?

**Peta:** Thanks, Deanna! Since you worked on the business proposal for this project, do you want to start by giving a general overview of the project idea for everyone?

**Deanna:** Sure! Sauce & Spoon would like to launch a pilot rollout of tabletop menu tablets at two restaurant locations: Sauce & Spoon North and Sauce & Spoon Downtown. Guests can place orders on the tablets as soon as they arrive instead of waiting for a server, which has been an issue in the past.

These two locations are appropriate for the pilot because each has the right staff size and guest volume to test the concept. Gilly and Alex are the general managers of these locations—do either of you have anything to add?

**Gilly (General Manager, North):** I’m excited that our location has been selected for this pilot. I was wondering which section of the restaurant we want to use for the tablet rollout. Will we be piloting the entire restaurant?

Personally, I think the bar area is ideal. It’s the section where we are always hoping to drive faster turnover. I also think guests in the bar area would appreciate a faster dining experience.

**Alex (General Manager, Downtown):** I agree, Gilly. The bar area is my pick for testing out the tablets, too.

**Deanna:** Great points—yes, we intended to pilot the tablets in only one section of each restaurant. Setting them up in the bar area makes sense.

**Peta:** Great. We’ll roll out the tablets in the bar section of each restaurant. Deanna, can you discuss some of the major outcomes you’re hoping to achieve with this project?

**Deanna:** One of our biggest company-wide goals is to increase our product mix. We think one way this project can help is by upselling appetizers or promoting certain entrees.

Seydou, I’ve seen on tablet-vendor websites that there is a feature to promote specific menu items. Does this seem fairly standard and like something we could incorporate?

**Seydou (Restaurant Consultant):** Yes. Several models allow you to suggest any menu item as an add-on, and you can display a coupon for that item as well.

**Deanna:** Excellent. Peta, we definitely want to try the menu-item add-on feature and coupons, so we’ll need to identify a tablet package with those features.

We also want to decrease our average table-turn time by about **30 minutes**, since delays in service have been a source of customer frustration. I know this is an ambitious goal, but I’m hopeful we can achieve it because guests can use the tablets to order immediately and pay their own checks without waiting for a server.

**Peta:** Got it. As someone new to the restaurant industry, I may need help with definitions. Can you clarify what you mean by table-turn time?

**Deanna:** Table-turn time is the amount of time a party occupies a table, including the time required to prepare the table for the next party. Our current average turn time is about **95 minutes** for most sections and **80 minutes** in the bar area.

**Peta:** Thanks. So, you think reducing average table-turn time by 30 minutes is a reasonable goal?

**Gilly:** Yes. Our dinner hours are from **5:00 p.m. to 10:00 p.m.**, and we believe each table should be able to serve at least four parties during that time.

We want guests to have a great experience, so we do not want them to feel rushed. However, we think the tablets can reduce the time guests spend waiting for tables and servers. Reducing table-turn time should also help with other goals, such as increasing average daily guest counts by **10%**.

**Peta:** I see. Thanks for sharing more context. Are there any other goals you would like to discuss?

**Alex:** At my location, we have noticed a growing amount of food waste. I think part of the reason is that we comp meals when customers send food back.

**Peta:** What does “comp” mean?

**Alex:** Comping means not charging the customer. Our current policy is to prepare new meals for guests who report that their orders were prepared incorrectly. This can become cumbersome and expensive if it happens too frequently.

**Gilly:** That has been a problem for us as well. I wonder if the tablets will help us communicate specific guest requests to the kitchen more directly. If a guest modifies a menu item, we will have a record of it.

**Peta:** Do you have a specific goal for reducing food waste?

**Alex:** Not yet. It would be helpful to get estimates of how much waste we can expect to reduce through this project and then establish a reasonable goal.

**Peta:** Okay, I’ll look into that. I’m adding the following items to the charter:

- Pilot the tablets in the bar areas
- Identify tablet packages that include menu add-ons and coupons
- Obtain estimates and establish a goal for reducing food waste

Is there anything you are worried about with the tablet rollout?

**Gilly:** Change can be challenging for staff. There is always a learning curve when adopting new technology, and employees can sometimes be resistant.

**Peta:** Yes, of course. We will include a staff-training plan for the new system. What else?

**Deanna:** It is not fully clear how the tablets will integrate with our existing POS system and host software.

**Peta:** Can you tell me more about what those software systems do?

**Deanna:** The host software tracks table usage and waitlists. The point-of-sale, or POS, software manages prices, tracks orders, and generates checks at the end of a meal.

The tablet software should be compatible with our existing systems, but we will need to configure them correctly to make the integration as seamless as possible.

**Seydou:** The account-services teams I have worked with have a lot of experience with this. Peta, let me know when you are ready to research tablet packages, and I can work with you on that.

**Peta:** Thanks, Seydou. That will be very helpful.

**Deanna:** Excellent. Peta, what other questions do you have?

**Peta:** I wanted to review the timeline for this rollout.

**Deanna:** We would like to start the pilot at the beginning of **Q2**, which gives us the next quarter to prepare. If we can do it sooner, that would be even better because January and February tend to be less busy.

**Peta:** This is great. Thanks, everyone, for answering my questions. I may have more later, but I think I have what I need for now. I’ll begin drafting a project charter that summarizes the project and explains the goals, scope, and future deliverables.
