# sauce-and-spoon-tablet-rollout-pm-portfolio

> A project management case study for a two-location restaurant tabletop-tablet rollout.

## Project Overview

Sauce & Spoon introduced tabletop menu tablets in the bar areas of its North and Downtown restaurant locations. The project was designed to improve the guest experience, streamline ordering and checkout, reduce food waste, increase operational efficiency, and support revenue growth.

This portfolio documents the project from initiation through closing. It includes the project charter, stakeholder-management work, scope decisions, task planning, duration estimates, quality standards, customer feedback, a retrospective, and closeout reporting.

## Project Goals

- Reduce average table-turn time by **30 minutes**.
- Increase average daily guest count by **10%**.
- Reduce food waste by **25%**.
- Increase average check value from **$65 to at least $75**.
- Increase appetizer sales by **15% overall**.
- Keep tablet checkout time at **one minute or less**.
- Keep customer-reported technical issues below **5% per week**.
- Improve customer satisfaction with the tablet experience.

## Results and Impact

The tablet rollout launched on **April 23** as planned, despite early project delays that required the team to accelerate later tasks.

- Increased daily guest count at the Downtown location by **20%**, exceeding the original 10% target.
- Reduced average table-turn time by **30 minutes** through waitstaff training, guest-pacing improvements, and tablet-supported ordering.
- Reduced food waste by **25%**, meeting the project goal.
- Maintained average tablet checkout time at **one minute or less**.
- Reduced weekly customer-reported technical issues to **below 5%** through pre-service testing and tablet-maintenance procedures.
- Increased customer satisfaction scores of 4 or 5 from **72% after the pilot** to **86% after launch**.
- Increased monthly revenue after tablet implementation; July revenue was approximately **20% higher than April revenue**.

## Key Lessons Learned

- Tablets alone did not reduce table-turn time. Staff training, guest pacing, and workflow improvements were also needed.
- The test launch and customer survey helped identify problems with navigation, payment messaging, technical reliability, and service flow before the official launch.
- Regular vendor check-ins helped the team manage task dependencies and maintain the planned launch date.
- Future rollouts should account for staff availability, location-specific workflows, back-of-house capacity, and training needs earlier in the planning process.
