# Closeout Report: Tablet Rollout

## Overview

This closeout report documents the completion of the Sauce & Spoon tabletop tablet rollout project.

The report was created by reviewing the customer survey results from the tablet test launch, the post-project launch check-in email exchange, and project documentation created throughout the project lifecycle. These materials provided information about customer experience, project performance, post-launch improvements, results achieved, lessons learned, and recommended next steps.

## Project Summary

Sauce & Spoon implemented tabletop menu tablets to improve the guest dining experience and restaurant operations. The rollout introduced tablets for guest ordering and card payments, with the goal of making service faster, improving order accuracy, reducing food waste, increasing daily guest counts, and reducing table-turn time. Key success criteria included reducing average table-turn time by 30 minutes, keeping tablet checkout time at one minute or less, limiting weekly customer-reported technical issues to less than 5%, increasing daily guest count by 10%, and reducing food waste by 25%.

---

## Methodology

The project used a hybrid project-management approach. Traditional planning practices were used to define the project charter, scope, goals, budget, stakeholders, milestones, and launch plan. Agile practices were used during the test launch and rollout to gather feedback, identify issues, make improvements, and monitor results.
1. **Initiation and planning:** The team created a project charter, identified goals, defined scope, documented stakeholders, established quality standards and developed a project plan.

2. **Test launch:** Sauce & Spoon conducted a test launch and collected guest feedback through a survey.

3. **Evaluation and adjustment:** The team reviewed survey results and operational performance data to identify issues with tablet navigation, table-turn time, payment messaging and technical reliability.

4. **Implementation:** The team applied improvements, trained waitstaff, introduced a pre-service tablet-testing checklist and adjusted guest communication.

5. **Monitoring and closeout:** The project team tracked performance against agreed-upon goals and documented outcomes for the closeout report. The project used agile project-management practices such as chartering, scope definition, milestone planning, stakeholder communication and closeout reporting while iterative improvements based on test-launch feedback and stakeholder/team discussions.

## Key Accomplishments

- Launched the tablet pilot on April 23 as planned, even though the team had to speed up some tasks after early delays.
- Stayed close to the original budget. Training, website/menu updates and maintenance costs were lower than expected.
- Installed tablets at both restaurant locations with support from an electrician.
- Added menus, coupons, branding and other restaurant content to the tablets.
- Connected the tablets to the POS system.
- Worked with the tablet vendor to keep the launch on schedule.
- Developed a training plan and trained both front-of-house and back-of-house staff.
- Addressed waitstaff questions and concerns during the rollout.
- Put tablet maintenance and security procedures in place.
- Created a customer survey and tracking process to measure the pilot’s results.
- Met or exceeded several project goals, including reducing table-turn time by 30 minutes, keeping checkout time to one minute or less, reducing technical complaints to under 5%, increasing Downtown guest counts by 20% and reducing food waste by 25%.

---

## Lessons Learned

- The project involved more work than expected. Electrical installation, POS integration, vendor coordination, tablet setup, staff training and maintenance all had to be completed before launch.
- Early delays meant the team had to move faster on later tasks to meet the April 23 launch date.
- The test launch and customer survey helped catch issues before the full rollout, including tablet navigation, payment instructions, technical problems and table-turn time.
- Tablets helped but they were not enough on their own to reduce table-turn time. Staff training and better attention to guest pacing also made a difference.
- Guests needed clearer information about payment options, especially because the tablets accepted cards while some guests wanted to pay with cash.
- The pre-service testing checklist helped the team find and remove tablets that were not working properly.
- Incorrect orders may happen at different points: when the order is placed, prepared in the kitchen or delivered to the table. All of these areas should be reviewed together.
- Future budgets should include more detailed estimates for hardware, subscriptions, installation and customization.

---

## Next Steps

- Keep tracking tablet performance, customer feedback, order accuracy, checkout time, food ticket time and technical complaints.
- Continue using the pre-service testing, maintenance and tablet-locking procedures.
- Continue coaching front-of-house staff on guest pacing, helping guests use tablets and explaining payment options.
- Review incorrect orders with both front-of-house and kitchen teams to find where errors are happening.
- Track the $3,600 annual hardware and software subscription cost and include it in future budgets.
- Use the actual project costs, timeline delays and task dependencies to improve planning for future rollouts.
- Review customer surveys and operational results before deciding whether to expand tablets beyond the two pilot locations.
- Have general managers oversee day-to-day tablet performance, with Operations and IT supporting training and technical issues.
