## Lessons Learned

- A project manager does not need to have every answer right away. The role often involves asking clear questions, gathering information, and helping the team make decisions.
- SMART goals help the team understand exactly what success looks like and how progress will be measured.
- Clearly defining scope early helps prevent confusion. When a stakeholder requests something outside scope, it is important to understand the request, explain the impact, and document the decision.
- Stakeholders may support the same project while having different priorities. Listening to each perspective and finding common ground is important.
- Good communication means more than sending updates. It means making sure stakeholders understand goals, responsibilities, deadlines, and decisions.
- Project tasks have dependencies. For example, staff cannot be trained on tablets until the tablets are installed and tested.
- Survey results, project metrics, and stakeholder feedback can show what is working and what needs to improve.
- I enjoyed project planning, stakeholder communication, problem-solving, and organizing information into clear project documents.
- I would like to keep developing my confidence with budgeting, scheduling, and project-management tools in a real work environment.
